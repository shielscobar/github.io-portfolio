Finite Element
Analysis

My approach to FEM begins with a rigorous understanding of the physical problem before any mesh is drawn. Load paths, boundary conditions, and failure modes are established analytically first — the simulation is a verification tool, not the starting point.

I work across linear static, non-linear, and modal analyses, with particular focus on composite laminates and thin-shell structures typical of F1 aero and chassis components. Mesh quality is treated with the same discipline as the results themselves.

Post-processing is structured around engineering decisions: margin plots, sensitivity sweeps, and correlation against physical test data where available.

  • — Pre-analysis: hand calculations, free-body diagrams, and load case definition before any model is built.
  • — Mesh strategy tailored to stress gradients — coarse where appropriate, refined at joints, fasteners, and ply drops.
  • — Composite-specific failure criteria: Tsai-Wu, Puck, and first-ply failure assessments for CFRP primary structure.
  • — Model correlation and validation against experimental strain gauge and DIC data from physical testing.

02

CATIA
Design

I design with manufacturing in mind from the first sketch. In CATIA V5/V6, my workflow is parametric by default — every dimension is driven by design intent, enabling rapid iteration without geometry rebuilds.

For composite components, I work directly with ply stacking definitions and draping simulations, ensuring what is modelled is what can be laid up. Surface quality and curvature continuity are treated as engineering constraints, not aesthetic choices.

I maintain clean, well-structured part trees and assembly hierarchies that survive revision cycles and hand-off to analysis, tooling, and manufacturing teams without loss of context.

  • — Parametric part and skeleton modelling for top-down assembly design.
  • — GSD surface modelling for aerodynamic surfaces, ensuring curvature continuity to Class A standards.
  • — Composites workbench: ply definition, zone-based design, and flattening for cutting patterns.
  • — Drawing and model-based definition (MBD) — PMI annotation for manufacturing without 2D drawings.

Production
Planning

Production planning in F1 is a constraint satisfaction problem — materials, layup sequences, cure cycles, tooling occupancy, and event deadlines form a tightly coupled system. I work across the full flow from works order release to sign-off.

I bring an engineer's understanding to the production floor: knowing which concessions are structurally acceptable, where schedule risk is real versus perceived, and how to communicate clearly between design, analysis, and composite shop.

  • — Works order generation and route card management for composite primary structure programmes.
  • — Autoclave and out-of-autoclave (OOA) cure scheduling — managing oven capacity against event cycles.
  • — NCR and concession management — technical assessment of non-conformances against allowable damage limits.
  • — Tooling coordination — liaison between design, composites, and CMM inspection through first article.

04

DIC
Tool

Digital Image Correlation is a full-field displacement and strain measurement technique that provides data no strain gauge grid can — surface strain fields, deformation modes, and crack initiation sites resolved spatially across a component under load.

I built a custom DIC processing and visualisation tool to complement commercial packages, addressing specific gaps in post-processing workflow for composite structural tests.

Custom DIC Processing Tool

A Python-based tool for post-processing DIC output data — importing displacement fields, computing derived strain tensors, overlaying results on component geometry, and correlating with FE predictions on a node-by-node basis.

Designed for structural test campaigns: outputs margin maps directly comparable to FEA, with automated reporting for test records and correlation matrices for model validation.
